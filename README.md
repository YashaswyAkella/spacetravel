# spacetravel
🌌 How Our Solar System Moves in Space

Project Overview
This project is an interactive HTML-based visualization that demonstrates how our solar system moves through space. It focuses on the Sun and its eight planets, showing how they orbit the Sun while the entire solar system travels through the Milky Way galaxy.

The goal is to help users understand that our solar system is not stationary. Everything is constantly moving through space!

☀️ The Solar System
Our solar system consists of the Sun and eight planets:

Mercury
Venus
Earth
Mars
Jupiter
Saturn
Uranus
Neptune
The Sun is at the center of our solar system and provides the gravitational force that keeps the planets in orbit.

🪐 How the Solar System Moves
There are several types of movement happening in our solar system:

1. Planetary Rotation
Each planet spins around its own axis. This movement is called rotation.

Earth takes approximately 24 hours to complete one rotation.
Jupiter rotates in about 10 hours.
Venus rotates very slowly, taking approximately 243 Earth days.
2. Planetary Revolution
Each planet travels around the Sun in an elliptical orbit. This movement is called revolution.

Mercury takes 88 Earth days to orbit the Sun.
Earth takes 365.25 days.
Neptune takes approximately 165 Earth years.
3. The Sun's Movement Through the Galaxy
The Sun is not stationary. It travels around the center of the Milky Way galaxy, carrying all eight planets along with it.

The Sun moves at approximately 220 km/s and takes roughly 225–250 million years to complete one orbit around the center of the galaxy. This journey is sometimes called a Galactic Year.

4. The Solar System's Journey Through Space
As the Sun travels through the Milky Way, the planets continue orbiting it. This means the entire solar system is moving together through space.

The planets follow the Sun while maintaining their orbits, creating a complex combination of movements.
